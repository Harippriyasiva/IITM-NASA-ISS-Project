COG Functional Category Distribution — Top 10 cumulative gene counts across 17 strains
Amino acid metabolism (E) and transcription (K) dominate — reflecting metabolic versatility and complex regulatory networks essential for ISS survival. Defense mechanisms (V) and mobilome (X) confirm ISS-specific adaptive pressure.
COG profile per strain — key functional categories
Horizontal grouped bars showing 11 COG categories per strain. Ralstonia strains show the broadest functional repertoire across all categories.
Average COG profile — ISS-relevant categories (radar)
Radar shows mean gene count per category across all 17 strains. Energy production, ion transport, and defense are notably elevated relative to typical environmental bacteria.
AMR & Resistance gene hits per strain — NCBI AMR · CARD · VFDB · BacMet (÷4 scaled)
Ralstonia insidiosa strains (8) consistently carry OXA-573 (carbapenem) and OXA-574 (β-lactam) genes. S. warneri 140720001-1 has the highest CARD hit count (5). Streptococcus sp. is the only strain with VFDB virulence hits (5 adherence factors). BacMet values scaled ÷4 for display — hover for true count.
CARD resistance class distribution
β-lactam/carbapenem resistance dominates (Ralstonia clade). Fluoroquinolone resistance detected in Staphylococci. Moenomycin and peptide resistance co-occur in multi-drug resistant strains.
BacMet — metal & biocide resistance genes (≥80% identity)
Mercury resistance (mer operon, blue) and chromate/heavy-metal efflux (chrA, cnrA, czcA, red) are most prevalent — consistent with ISS metal surfaces and cleaning biocide exposure.

All 5 virulence genes are adherence factors — pavA (fibronectin binding), sipA/srtG1/srtG2 (pilus assembly PI-2), psaA (Mn-transport/adhesion). No toxin-encoding genes detected across the 17 novel strains.
BGC count per strain — antiSMASH (total 94 clusters across 17 strains)
Ralstonia insidiosa (8 strains) and Methylorubrum extorquens each carry 8 BGCs — the highest biosynthetic potential. Kocuria spp. carry 5 each. NI-siderophore clusters are conserved across multiple genera, suggesting iron acquisition is a shared ISS-adaptive strategy.
BGC type distribution — all 94 clusters (polar area)
NI-siderophore (14), terpene (12), and betalactone (10) dominate. T1PKS, arylpolyene, butyrolactone, and hydrogen-cyanide (8 each) reflect genus-level conservation. NRPS clusters (2) indicate potential for novel peptide bioactives.
High-confidence BGCs — ≥50% MIBiG similarity (13 clusters)

3 BGCs reach 100% similarity: staphyloferrin A (siderophore) in both S. warneri strains, and carotenoid terpene in M. extorquens — functionally confirmed. 8 Ralstonia strains share an alcaligin-like siderophore cluster at 62% similarity.
Plasmid sequences per strain — geNomad
R. insidiosa 121560039-2 (17 plasmids) and 123250053-1 (14 plasmids) have the highest plasmid burden. Rothia mucilaginosa is the only plasmid-free strain. High plasmid counts in Ralstonia suggest active horizontal gene transfer.
Viral/prophage sequences per strain — geNomad
All 25 viral sequences are Caudoviricetes (dsDNA tailed phages). S. warneri 121210007-3 carries 4 viral sequences — the highest. 2 strains (Sphingomonas, Rothia) are entirely phage-free.
Plasmid functional classification — AMR & conjugation marker prevalence
Of 121 plasmid sequences: 56 carry AMR marker genes and 35 carry conjugation genes — indicating active horizontal gene transfer capacity within the ISS microbiome.
Mobilome burden — plasmid vs viral sequences (bubble = plasmid size)
The Ralstonia clade (purple) dominates both axes. Bubble radius scales with plasmid count. Strains in the top-right combine high plasmid and viral burden — maximum genomic plasticity.
